## 1. Formal framing corrections

A general rule that now governs the whole body of the audit, retroactively:

> **Access restriction, generic warning and interested testimony are not — singly or together — proof of cover-up or of prior knowledge.**

Each of those three has its own limited evidentiary value: access restriction is a fact about the *process* of an investigation; a generic warning is evidence that a *category* of threat was known; interested testimony is a claim that gains weight from an oath and loses it from interest. None converts into proof of a deliberate act without independent corroboration.

### 1.1 The three-dates rule

Every documentary citation in this audit must now distinguish:

| Date | What it is | Error it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **Event date** | When the reported fact occurred (meeting, decision, source report) | Confusing the report with the fact |
| **Document date** | When the record was produced | Treating memory reconstructed years later as a contemporaneous record |
| **Declassification date** | When the record became public | **Treating a "new document" as a "new fact of 2001"** |

Applied to the three documents read in Phase 1:

| Doc | Event | Document | Declassification | Event → document distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DOC-1 — *Report on Review of PDB Articles* | PDBs from 1998 to 20/09/2001 (corpus reviewed) | 09/02/2004 | 08/09/2026 | 2.5 to 6 years |
| DOC-2 — MFR Condoleezza Rice | 07/02/2004 (the meeting); facts reported from 2000–2001 | 07/02/2004 (working note) | 08/09/2026 | ~0 for the meeting; 2.5–4 years for the facts |
| DOC-3 — MFR Mike Scheuer | 11/12/2003 (the testimony); facts reported from 1996–1999 | 11/12/2003 | 08/09/2026 | ~0 for the testimony; 4–7 years for the facts |

**Direct consequence.** None of the three documents is a contemporaneous record of the 1998–2001 facts it describes. DOC-2 and DOC-3 are declared memory at a distance of 2.5 to 7 years from the fact; DOC-1 is a synthesis produced in 2004 about documents of 1998–2001. The 2026 declassification made public **records of 2003–2004**, not operational documents of 2001. Where evidentiary value depends on contemporaneity — and in priorities 2, 3 and 6 below it does — what matters is the contemporaneous document *cited* within the testimony, not the testimony.

### 1.2 H1 — formulation replaced

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before (Phase 1)** | "H1 reinforced, and earlier than supposed. […] More likely than not." |
| **Now** | **"There are documented indications of bureaucratic failures and possible institutional self-protection; the specific claim of deliberate suppression is not yet corroborated."** |

Reason for the change: the two pillars of the earlier formulation do not bear the weight they carried. The episode of the PDB of 25/03/1999 is a claim under oath ⁽ᵃ⁾ by a witness with a declared interest, whose supporting memorandum has been neither located nor read. Rice's retraction is the retraction of a **public sentence**, not evidence of information suppression. And the structure of PDB access is a fact about the Commission's process in 2004 — subsequent to the attack, internal to an investigation, and with no logical bearing on suppression in 1999–2001.

### 1.3 H5 — formulation replaced

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before (Phase 1)** | "Unlikely on what was read; untested where it matters." |
| **Now** | **"No positive evidence of H5 was found in this tranche; it remains untested at the central al-Mihdhar/al-Hazmi node."** |

Reason for the change: "unlikely" is a statement about probability that would require having examined where the hypothesis has its best case. That was not examined. The absence of a consolidated analytical product described by Scheuer is evidence about CIA's analytical process in the period he covers — it is not a test of H5. Absence of positive evidence in documents that do not deal with the node is not evidence of absence.

### 1.4 Rice, "all of the reporting pointed abroad" — reclassified

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before (Phase 1)** | "Contradicted in its absolute form; defensible on a reading restricted to the summer of 2001." |
| **Now** | **"Unresolved documentary tension."** |

Closing the question in either direction requires four items, none of them available in the sources read:

1. **The full PDB of 06/08/2001** — what exists in the audit's record is the description the Commission made of it in DOC-1, not the text. A PDF with no text layer (recorded in `02`, DOC-6) is not a reading.
2. **The precise chronology of the summer of 2001** — month by month, not the 1998–2001 aggregate. Rice's statement is plausibly about the June–August *threat spike*; the corpus of 353 articles covers three years.
3. **The reliability attributed to the sources** — DOC-1 itself records that several core-group reports came from sources CIA classified as "highly questionable, unreliable", and that the PDB of 06/08/2001 carried, in bold in the original, a non-corroboration caveat. "There were reports" and "there were reports the service considered reliable" are different propositions.
4. **The actual distribution of warnings between domestic and foreign** — DOC-1 gives the thematic cut of 24 articles, not the distribution of the corpus. Without a denominator, "the stream pointed abroad" is not testable.

### 1.5 The Commission's access structure — inference withdrawn

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before (Phase 1)** | "No commissioner other than Kean and Hamilton, and no thematic team, had direct access to the PDB corpus […] independent verification by other commissioners was structurally impossible." |
| **Now** | **Proven** as to the structure of access to the PDB corpus. **Not determined** as to the other commissioners' access to derivative material. **Not established** that independent verification was impossible. |

The memorandum establishes how many people saw the PDBs. It does not establish what the other people saw by other routes. These are four distinct determinations, all pending — see section 4, priority 5.

## 4. Phase 2A priorities — pre-registered questions

Pre-registering the question before reading the document is what prevents the post-hoc adjustment that Phase 1 identified as a methodological failure in audit `01`. Each priority below defines **what a positive, a negative and an inconclusive answer would be** before any reading.

### Priority 1 — The al-Mihdhar/al-Hazmi node

The point where H5 has its best theoretical case and which remains **entirely untouched**.

- **Targets:** MFRs of Tenet (×3) and Clarke (×3); Scheuer #2 (06/01/2004) and #3 (11/03/2004); Joint Inquiry; DOJ IG reports; PENTTBOM.
- **Question:** who knew, on what date, that al-Mihdhar held a valid US visa and that al-Hazmi had entered the United States; why the information was not passed to the FBI or put on a watchlist between January 2000 and August 2001; and is there a contemporaneous record — not 2003–2004 memory — of a decision to **withhold** the information.
- **Positive answer for H5:** a contemporaneous document showing a deliberate decision not to pass it on, with identified authorship and no declared operational justification.
- **Negative answer:** a contemporaneous record of process failure (cable not sent, draft not approved, wrong channel), or an operational justification declared at the time.
- **Inconclusive:** only declared memory years afterwards, with no contemporaneous document located — which is the current state.

### Priority 2 — The 30% vs 0% discrepancy (capture operation, May 1998)

- **Targets:** MFRs of Tenet and Berger; MFRs of Reno, Freeh, White and Fitzgerald (**not** part of the 2026 release — location pending in NARA's general MFR holdings).
- **Question:** do the two numbers exist in a contemporaneous record, or only in Scheuer's memory in December 2003? Who communicated which estimate to whom, and on what date?
- **Discriminating test:** if Tenet or Berger describe the same meeting with an estimate other than 30%, the claim becomes `contradita` or `parcialmente corroborada` (partially corroborated) depending on the detail; if neither mentions numbers, it remains `declaração não corroborada` (uncorroborated statement).

### Priority 3 — The allegedly withheld PDB of 25/03/1999

- **Targets:** the memorandum cited by Scheuer; the author of the barred text, named in the MFR; MFRs of Tenet.
- **Methodological trap to avoid:** the published PDB of 25/03/1999 — if it is among the 71 released — **cannot** confirm or refute the claim, because the claim is about a text said to have been *removed before publication*. An item absent from a PDB is compatible both with "it was barred" and with "it was never written". The real test is the internal memorandum and the testimony of the named author.
- **Current status:** `documento citado, não localizado` (document cited, not located).

### Priority 4 — The PDB of 06/08/2001

- **Target:** the PDB's PDF on the CIA page. Technical obstacle already documented: a scan with no text layer. Requires OCR — therefore requires access to the file.
- **Question:** does the full text sustain Rice's characterisation ("no current information"; the contact with the UAE embassy as a hoax) or the Commission's description in DOC-1 (70 FBI field investigations; CIA and FBI investigating the contact)?
- **Observation:** both things can be true at once. "Historical information, not current" and "there are 70 open investigations" do not necessarily contradict each other. The test is the text.

### Priority 5 — The structure of the Commission's access to the PDBs

Four distinct determinations, all pending:

| # | Determination | State | Where it is resolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5a | Who exactly were the **four** members of the Review Team | Not determined. DOC-1 records that it included the Chair and Vice-Chair; it does **not name** the four | DOC-1 re-read with attention to signatures and distribution list; Commission–White House correspondence |
| 5b | Who were the **two** subcommittee members with access to the full corpus | Not determined | Idem |
| 5c | Which commissioners and thematic teams had access to **NID/SEIB, MFRs, briefings and derivative documents** | Not determined — and this is what decides whether independent verification was possible | Commission minutes and internal rules; thematic-team MFRs; the Kean/Hamilton holdings |
| 5d | Whether there was a **formal mechanism for sharing** the Review Team's findings with the full Commission | Not determined | The Commission's internal rules; DOC-1 itself, which is formally a *report* — to whom was it addressed? |

**Secondary-source lead, unverified and not promoted to fact:** contemporary reporting indicates that the agreement with the White House provided for a restricted group that would brief the full Commission, and mentions Philip Zelikow (Executive Director) and Commissioner Jamie Gorelick as the two with expanded access. This was **not** confirmed in a primary document in this session and is recorded only as a search direction. If confirmed, it would have a direct consequence: the expanded access would include a commissioner outside the Kean/Hamilton pair and the Executive Director — which would make the Phase 1 formulation wrong in the names, not merely in the scope. Note also that Zelikow is the same person whose apparent conflict of interest with Rice (co-authorship of a book in 1995) Phase 1 identified; if he is one of the two, that is relevant to the independence audit, and not to H5.

### Priority 6 — CIA–FBI interaction and the foreign-intelligence / domestic-investigation gap

- **Targets:** MFRs of Tenet and Clarke; Joint Inquiry; DOJ IG reports.
- **Question:** was the gap (i) a legal barrier ("the wall"), (ii) institutional practice beyond what the barrier required, or (iii) a case-by-case decision to withhold? The three produce the same observable result and have completely different implications for H1, H4 and H5.
- **Why it matters:** this is the distinction that separates H4 from H5 in the concrete case. Treating them as one was the category error Phase 1 made with the four demolition hypotheses and which must not be repeated here.

## 5. Claim-by-claim verification instrument

Closed status vocabulary. No other term is admitted in this column:

`declaração não corroborada` · `documento citado, não localizado` · `documento localizado, não lido` · `parcialmente corroborada` · `corroborada` · `contradita` · `inconclusiva`

*(Glosses: uncorroborated statement · document cited, not located · document located, not read · partially corroborated · corroborated · contradicted · inconclusive. The tokens are kept in Portuguese as identifiers — see the translation note at the head of this file.)*

Rules of application:

- `corroborada` requires a **source independent of the witness**, read in full. A witness corroborating himself in another statement corroborates nothing.
- `documento citado, não localizado` is the default status of every claim that rests on a memorandum, cable or report the audit has not located — even under oath, even with a date and an addressee.
- `contradita` requires that the contradicting source have been read in full. Tension between two descriptions not read in parallel is `inconclusiva`, not `contradita`.

## 6. Phase 2B — Joint Inquiry, Part Four: pre-registered protocol

**Not executed.** `intelligence.senate.gov` blocked (section 3). The protocol below is pre-registered for the session with access.

### 6.1 Rule of treatment

> The "28 pages" are **not** proof of Saudi state participation, and will not be treated as such in any circumstance.

The document is, by construction, a chapter about **information not pursued to the end** — an inventory of what the Joint Inquiry found and did not resolve. An item not pursued is a pending lead, not a suppressed conclusion. Three distinctions the protocol keeps separate on every line:

1. **An individual with a government link ≠ the government.** Employment, a stipend or a consular credential establish a link, not authorisation.
2. **Contact ≠ support ≠ conscious support for the attack.** Each step requires its own evidence.
3. **An investigative lead ≠ an investigative conclusion.** Part Four records mostly the first kind; saying which is which is the product of the reading.

### 6.2 Classification instrument

Each item in Part Four receives exactly one category:

| Category | Operational definition | Evidentiary weight |
|---|---|---|
| **lead** | A line of investigation recorded, with no declared resolution | Directs future search. Zero as proof |
| **statement** | An assertion by a person, identified or not, with no documentary corroboration in the text | Depends on identification, interest and corroboration |
| **document** | A record cited with date and origin | Weight according to contemporaneity and provenance |
| **corroborated fact** | An assertion the text declares verified by two or more independent sources | The highest available in the document |
| **judicial allegation** | An allegation from a court filing (*In re Terrorist Attacks* and related) | An allegation by an interested party. **Never** treated as an investigative finding |
| **investigative conclusion** | An explicit judgement by the Joint Inquiry itself | The weight of the body, limited by what the body declares it verified |

Each line also records: whether the Joint Inquiry declared the item **resolved, unresolved or not pursued**; and whether there is any indication that the FBI or CIA followed up.

### 6.3 Pre-registered questions

At the end of the reading, three questions, answered only with what the text sustains:

1. How many items are a `corroborated fact` or an `investigative conclusion`, against how many are a `lead` or a `statement`?
2. Does any item establish **authorisation or knowledge by an authority of the Saudi government**, as opposed to conduct by individuals with a government link?
3. Is what Part Four says about al-Bayoumi, al-Thumairy and Bassnan conscious support for the attack, documented contact, or a lead not pursued — item by item?

**None of these questions is answered in this session.** H2 and H3 remain where Phase 1 left them: genuinely unresolved.
