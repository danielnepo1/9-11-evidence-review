## Governing principle

> “Every material claim is provisional until supported by traceable evidence. Official status does not make a claim true; institutional inconsistency does not make an alternative claim true. The project applies skepticism symmetrically: it actively tests the official account for contradictions and unexplained anomalies, while applying the same burden of proof, alternative-explanation testing, and falsification criteria to every alternative hypothesis.”

## Step 2 — Official Account Stress-Test Track

**Purpose:** identify and rigorously test the highest-yield points of tension in the official narrative — and record, with equal rigour, where the official narrative survives the test.

**Method, without exception:** every official claim entering this track is decomposed into atomic propositions and recorded in [`docs/claim-decomposition-register.md`](docs/claim-decomposition-register.md) with all twelve fields of [`docs/04`](docs/04-methodology-symmetric-skepticism.md) §4.1 — exact claim; responsible institution or witness; original source and date; source character (contemporaneous / retrospective / sworn / technical / secondary); later revisions, retractions or conflicting accounts; assumptions required for the claim to hold; supporting evidence; contradicting or weakening evidence; plausible non-conspiratorial explanations; plausible alternative explanations; the single most discriminating missing document, test or data point; status.

Every point of tension found is opened as an entry in [`docs/anomaly-register.md`](docs/anomaly-register.md) and carries the mirror test M1–M5 before any status is fixed.

**Constraint carried into every workstream:** an anomaly is a research lead. Finding one does not move H1–H7. Only positive evidence for the mechanism a hypothesis asserts moves that hypothesis (`04` §3, S2–S4).

## Step 3 — Mirror testing of H1–H7 against the same evidence

Runs on the output of step 2, using **the same evidence set** — no hypothesis is tested against a private corpus.

For each of H1–H7, and for each decomposed official proposition it bears on:

1. State what the hypothesis predicts that H0 does not.
2. Identify which register entries would be expected to exist if it were true, and check whether they do.
3. Apply the burden rule (`04` §3, S6): what the hypothesis asserts sets the bar, not who asserts it.
4. Record explicitly what would **falsify** it, and whether that observation is obtainable.
5. Record what it would take to move it, and what it currently is not supported by.

**Output:** a hypothesis-by-hypothesis assessment in which each status cites register IDs. A hypothesis whose support consists only of gaps, opacity or distrust is recorded as unsupported, with that fact stated plainly. Equally, an official proposition whose support consists only of institutional assertion is recorded as `unsupported statement`, with that fact stated plainly.

## Reporting format required in every phase from here on

Every phase document must contain these four sections, in this order:

1. **Findings that challenge or weaken the official account**
2. **Findings that challenge or weaken alternative accounts**
3. **What remains unresolved**
4. **What evidence would change the assessment**

Sections 1 and 2 are both mandatory even when empty; an empty section is written out as such and is itself a result. Section 3 cites register IDs and distinguishes *unresolved because untested* from *unresolved because tested and indeterminate*. Section 4 names specific obtainable items and states which way each would move which hypothesis.

## Execution rule

Every new phase follows the same standard: record full title, author, event date, document date, declassification date, URL, version, the extent actually read, redactions and access limitations — **before** any conclusion. No document is cited as "read" if only its summary, FAQ, press release or a search snippet was consulted.

And the framing rules, which apply in every phase and in both directions: access restriction, generic warning and interested testimony are not proof of a cover-up or of prior knowledge. Process failure, institutional self-protection, deliberate suppression and operational prior knowledge are four distinct propositions, each with its own burden of proof. Absence of a test is a limitation, never a result.
