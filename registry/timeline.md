# Timeline Register — Six Tracks

> Living instrument. Governed by [`../docs/04-controlling-methodology.md`](../docs/04-controlling-methodology.md) §4.1.
> Opened 19 September 2026.

---

## 1. Why four dates were not enough

The repository's four-date rule (event · document · annotation · declassification) is a **provenance** instrument: it tells you how far a record sits from the fact it reports. It is not a **chronology** instrument, and it cannot represent the questions a chronology raises — when something was observed as against when it was communicated, whether a timestamp was generated by a machine or reconstructed by a person years later, or what the precision of "approximately 9:40" actually is.

DISC-009 exposed the gap: one 13-page document dates the same meeting to both 10 and 15 March 2000, and the existing instruments could only record that as a discrepancy, not analyse it.

## 2. The six tracks

Every consequential event is recorded on whichever of these tracks the evidence supports, and **the tracks are never merged**:

| # | Track | What it records |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Event time** | When the thing happened |
| 2 | **Observation time** | When someone perceived it |
| 3 | **Communication time** | When it was transmitted |
| 4 | **Record creation time** | When the record of it was made |
| 5 | **Later recollection time** | When someone recalled it afterwards |
| 6 | **Declassification / publication time** | When the record became available |

Plus, per entry:

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| Time zone | Explicit; never assumed |
| Clock source | Named system, wall clock, witness estimate, or unknown |
| Precision | To the minute, hour, day, month — stated, not implied |
| Synchronisation evidence | What establishes that this clock agrees with others, or `none` |
| Generated or reconstructed | **`automatic`** (machine-generated at the time) or **`reconstructed`** (assigned later by a person) |

### 2.1 The rule that does the most work

> **Never combine "approximately", rounded media times and synchronised system logs without uncertainty bounds.**

A witness's "around 9:40" and a log's `09:40:12` are not the same datum and must never be averaged, matched, or presented in one column as though they were.

A corollary, from the four-date rule: **a record creation time is not an event time.** Treating the two as one is the most common way a chronology acquires false precision.

## 3. Entries

### TL-001 — The Millennium after-action review (DISC-009)

The register's first entry, and the reason it was built.

| Track | Value | Zone | Precision | Clock source | Generated / reconstructed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Event time | **Contested: 10 March 2000 or 15 March 2000** | unstated | day | witness reading from a briefing binder | **reconstructed** |
| Event time (related) | 12 March 2000 — DCI meets National Security Adviser on CT funding | unstated | day | same | **reconstructed** |
| Record creation | 28 January 2004 (DOC-8) | unstated | day | staff synthesis | reconstructed |
| Later recollection | 22 January 2004 (DOC-7 p.19) and 28 January 2004 (DOC-8 p.3) | unstated | day | witness | reconstructed |
| Declassification | 8 September 2026 | — | day | ISCAP stamp | automatic |

**Conflict.** DOC-8 printed p.3 gives "The March 10th, 2000 after-action report at NSC" and, later on the same page, "at the March 10th meeting". DOC-8 printed p.9 gives "the March 15th 2000 after action Millennium review". **Both readings are image-verified.**

**What the tracks show that a flat date field could not.** Every value on the event-time track is `reconstructed`, four years after the fact, by a witness reading from a document prepared for him. There is no `automatic` timestamp anywhere in this entry. The five-day conflict is therefore not a puzzle requiring explanation — it is the expected precision of the only clock source available. Recorded at **level 1, P3** in [`anomalies.md`](anomalies.md).

**Discriminating item:** the NSC after-action report itself, which would supply an `automatic` record-creation time. Not located.

### Standing scope note

The register is opened with one entry deliberately. Populating it is pulled by registry rows, like every other retrieval ([`source-policy.md`](source-policy.md) I1) — not by the availability of dates. Phase 4 (FAA/NORAD) is the first body of work that will require it at scale, and it is not begun.
