## 1. Controlled taxonomy (closed)

Each discrepancy takes exactly **one** primary category. Secondary categories may be listed but never substitute for the primary.

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| `direct contradiction` | Two sources assert propositions that cannot both be true |
| `chronological conflict` | Incompatible sequencing or timing of the same events |
| `changed account` | The same source's account altered over time |
| `material omission` | A source omits something it would be expected to address |
| `difference of scope` | The sources address different questions, periods or objects |
| `ambiguity` | The wording admits more than one reading |
| `uncertainty inflation` | A hedged statement later restated as settled |
| `source laundering` | A claim acquires apparent authority by republication without new evidence |
| `circular corroboration` | Apparent independent support that traces to one root |
| `inaccessible source` | The deciding material cannot be reached |
| `redaction-dependent claim` | The deciding material is withheld under classification |
| `conflict between witnesses` | Two witnesses give incompatible accounts of the same event |
| `internal agency disagreement` | Disagreement recorded within one institution |
| `possible administrative error` | Plausibly a clerical, drafting or filing mistake |
| `unresolved evidentiary gap` | No source currently addresses the question |

### 1.1 Distinctions that are never collapsed

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| "Not found in the reviewed corpus" | ≠ | "did not exist" |
| "Unsupported by disclosed evidence" | ≠ | "false" |
| "Officially asserted" | ≠ | "independently corroborated" |

### DISC-001 — Strategic analytic product vs. strategic plan before mid-1999

**Primary category:** `difference of scope`. Secondary: `unresolved evidentiary gap`.

**The two accounts.** DOC-3 (Scheuer, sworn, 11 Dec 2003): there was no strategic analytic product and no NIE on UBL attack methods until mid-1999, and he repeatedly asked for one. DOC-7 (Tenet, sworn, 22 Jan 2004, printed pp. 1, 16): after the 1998 embassy bombings "CIA had a strategic framework to address the elements of the al Qaeda threat, but in 1999, a new strategic plan was required ('The Plan')"; and "contrary to the Joint Inquiry's conclusions, Tenet insisted that there was a plan for ramping up war-like activities."

**EC analysis.**

| Class | Predicts | Would be weakened by | Distinguishing evidence | Currently available | Inaccessible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EC1** (different questions) | The two men are describing different objects — an *analytic* product vs. an *operational* plan; both statements true | A document showing the DCI treating "The Plan" as the analytic product Scheuer asked for | The text of "The Plan"; the NIC production record | Neither | Both |
| **EC2** (ordinary error / disagreement) | Genuine intra-agency disagreement about what counted as a strategic product | Contemporaneous agreement on terminology | Scheuer's 28 Jun 1999 memo and any reply to it | The memo's existence is asserted, not shown | The memo |
| **EC3** (self-protection) | The DCI overstates what existed; the former station chief overstates what was missing. **Both incentives run the same direction here** | Either man conceding the other's point | Contemporaneous production records, not recollection | None | Records |
| **EC4** (deliberate reframing) | Would require the disagreement to postdate a known public narrative need | Nothing yet suggests this | A drafting record showing the wording chosen against contrary knowledge | None | — |
| **EC5** (named alternative) | Not raised by any identified critic in the current corpus | — | — | — | — |

**Compatible with more than one class?** Yes — EC1, EC2 and EC3 all fit the present evidence. Nothing in the corpus distinguishes them.
**Current verdict:** unresolved. A1.2 sits at `inconclusiva`.

### DISC-002 — The sub-30% estimate and who was told

**Primary category:** `difference of scope`. Secondary: `redaction-dependent claim`.

**The two accounts.** DOC-3: 30% was conveyed to Berger, Reno, Freeh and Clarke; 0% to Mary Jo White. DOC-7 printed p.3 (image-verified): the prospect of success "as described to him was less than 30% (the figure agreed upon [REDACTED 25X1]"; printed p.4 (image-verified): Tenet "called Berger to inform him of his operational decision—not to debate the issue", and "did not tell the Principals' Committee why he cancelled the operation".

**What is actually in tension.** Not the number — both witnesses place a figure near or below 30% inside CIA. What is in tension is the *direction of flow*: Scheuer describes a figure briefed outward to four named policymakers; Tenet describes a figure described inward to him, and a notification to Berger that deliberately excluded the operational reasoning.

**EC analysis.**

| Class | Predicts | Would be weakened by | Distinguishing evidence | Currently available | Inaccessible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EC1** (different questions) | Both true: the figure circulated among CIA and the prosecutors independently of Tenet's call to Berger | Berger recalling being given a percentage by the DCI | MFR Berger; contemporaneous briefing records | Neither | Both |
| **EC2** (memory failure / fragmentation) | One or both men misremember a 5½-year-old conversation | A contemporaneous record matching one account exactly | The cancellation memorandum — **which the decision-maker does not know exists** (T-03) | None | The memorandum |
| **EC3** (self-protection) | Tenet minimizes what policymakers were told, to keep the decision inside his operational remit; Scheuer maximizes it, to show policymakers were engaged and the operation was viable | Either account conceding against interest. *Note:* Tenet's p.4 account **does** concede that he withheld reasoning from the Principals — against interest in one respect | A contemporaneous record of the Berger call | None | — |
| **EC4** (deliberate reframing) | Would require the 30%/0% split to have been constructed. Nothing in the corpus supports this | Any contemporaneous document showing a single consistent figure | MFRs of White and Fitzgerald | None | Both, outside the 2026 release |
| **EC5** (named alternative) | Not raised in the current corpus | — | — | — | — |

**Compatible with more than one class?** Yes. EC1 and EC2 are both fully consistent with everything now available; EC3 is consistent and unfalsified in either direction.
**Current verdict:** A3.1 `parcialmente corroborada`; A3.2 `inconclusiva`; A3.3 and A3.4 untouched. The redacted attribution on DOC-7 p.3 is the single item that would most reduce the space.

### DISC-003 — Whether a Saudi initiative bore on the May 1998 cancellation

**Primary category:** `conflict between witnesses`. Secondary: `redaction-dependent claim`, `difference of scope`.

**The two accounts.** DOC-3: Scheuer's stated **belief** that one reason for the cancellation was a Saudi offer to resolve the UBL case themselves. DOC-7 printed p.4 (image-verified): "Tenet remarked that his contemporaneous trip to Saudi Arabia (May 1998) was entirely unrelated to the cancellation of the capture operation"; the cancellation followed "the strong and unanimous recommendation" of three named senior operations officers; and "In the summer of 1998, Tenet did not think the USG was putting much faith in the Saudi initiative to persuade the Taliban to give up UBL".

**Scope note, load-bearing.** The object differs: Scheuer describes the Saudis resolving the UBL case *themselves*; Tenet describes a Saudi initiative to persuade the *Taliban*. These may be the same initiative described differently, or two initiatives. Nothing in the corpus settles which.

**The redaction.** Approximately 60% of the page on which Tenet's Saudi answer appears is a single 25X1 block, **invisible in the extracted text layer and visible only on the page image**. Whatever bears on this discrepancy in that block is unread. Any statement about what DOC-7 "does not say" about the Saudis is a statement about the disclosed 40% of that page.

**EC analysis.**

| Class | Predicts | Would be weakened by | Distinguishing evidence | Currently available | Inaccessible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EC1** (different questions) | Scheuer describes background context; Tenet describes the proximate decision rationale. Both true | Scheuer having claimed the Saudi offer was the *proximate* cause | The cancellation memorandum (T-03) | Neither | The memorandum |
| **EC2** (memory / fragmentation) | Scheuer, two levels below the decision, inferred a Saudi link from timing (the trip and the cancellation coincided in May 1998) | Scheuer citing a document rather than belief — he cites none | A contemporaneous record of the decision rationale | None | — |
| **EC3** (self-protection) | The DCI, whose decision this was and who was criticized for it, would deny a diplomatic motive for an operational call. **This incentive is direct and strong** | Tenet conceding any non-operational factor | The three officers' recommendation as written | None | — |
| **EC4** (deliberate reframing) | The Saudi element removed from the official account to protect a liaison relationship | The 25X1 block containing a full Saudi discussion consistent with the disclosed text | **The redacted block itself** | No | **Yes — this is the discriminating item, and it is classified** |
| **EC5** (named alternative) | Scheuer is the identified source; his stated basis is belief, not evidence | Any documentary basis being produced | Same as EC2 | None | — |

**Compatible with more than one class?** Yes, all five remain live. EC4 cannot be assessed at all while the block is withheld — which is itself the finding, and is **not** evidence that EC4 is true.
**Current verdict:** A5.2 `contradita` — the proposition as stated is negated by the decision-maker's sworn, image-verified account. A5.1 `parcialmente corroborada`. A4.2 `contradita` in its absolute form; A4.5 `parcialmente corroborada`. **None of this establishes that the cancellation decision was sound, that the Saudi relationship was unproblematic, or that the record is complete.**

### DISC-005 — The February 1999 MON: what the President's edit did

**Primary category:** `internal agency disagreement`. Secondary: `ambiguity`.

**What the document records.** DOC-7 printed p.11 (text layer): CIA's draft language — "if a successful capture operation is not feasible, we would request that you undertake offensive operations to kill Bin Ladin with his principal lieutenants" — was replaced in President Clinton's hand with "We understand that capture may not be possible and that Bin Ladin and his lieutenants may be killed in the confrontation." Tenet's reading: the edit returned the MON to 'capture preferred', i.e. **less** latitude. CIA General Counsel Muller: the President's changes made clear CIA could not tell Masood to kill UBL. And, recorded in the MFR as a bracketed editorial note: *"Commissioner Ben-Veniste said that he would interpret the NSC's note differently, i.e. that killing was still allowed."*

**Why this is in the ledger.** It is a disagreement about the meaning of a primary instrument, recorded **inside** an official document, between the witness and a commissioner — the sort of internal disagreement that a published synthesized narrative typically resolves silently in one direction. Preserving it unresolved is the point.

**EC analysis.**

| Class | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **EC1** | Live: Tenet reads the MON; Ben-Veniste reads the accompanying NSC note. Different instruments |
| **EC2** | Live: genuine legal ambiguity in a hand-edited authorization |
| **EC3** | Live in both directions: the DCI has an interest in a narrow reading (explaining non-action); a commissioner probing non-action has an interest in a broad one |
| **EC4** | Not assessable — neither instrument is in the corpus |
| **EC5** | Not raised by an identified critic in the current corpus |

**Discriminating evidence:** the February 1999 MON and the NSC note, neither located. Both appear on the document's own follow-up list (item 9).
**Current verdict:** T-08 `documento citado, não localizado`. The disagreement is preserved, not resolved.

### DISC-006 — DOC-7 carries annotations that postdate its own document date

**Primary category:** `possible administrative error`. Secondary: `changed account`, `ambiguity`.

**Observation.** DOC-7 is dated 22 January 2004. It contains at least three internal cross-references to other sessions, at least one of which necessarily postdates it:

| Location | Text as extracted | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Printed p.12, footnote 1 | "In the DCI's follow-up session with the Commission, on January 28[th], 2004…" | Six days **after** the document's own date |
| Printed p.13, footnote 2 | "In the Commission's January 1[5?]th, 2004 interview with DCI Tenet…" | Digit OCR-uncertain. No 15 Jan 2004 Tenet MFR appears in the ISCAP 2026-201 index |
| Printed p.19, bracketed note | "Tenet noted for the record in his **December 28, 2004** interview with the Commission…" | **Image-verified: the printed text does read 2004.** Not an OCR artifact. Chronologically impossible if read literally; the Commission's Tenet interviews in the index are 23 Dec 2003, 22 Jan 2004 and 28 Jan 2004 |

**Consequence for the three-dates rule.** [`../docs/03` §1.1](../docs/03-phase2a-mfr-verification.md) requires every citation to distinguish event date, document date and declassification date. DOC-7 shows that **"document date" is not necessarily a single date**: this MFR is a layered artifact, drafted on or about 22 Jan 2004 and annotated later by Commission staff. Citing a passage to "22 January 2004" may therefore be wrong for the annotations. The rule is hereby extended: where a document carries internal evidence of later annotation, the annotation's own date is recorded separately, or marked `annotation date unknown`.

**EC analysis.**

| Class | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **EC1** | Not applicable — this is a property of one document, not a conflict between sources |
| **EC2** | **Most likely.** Routine staff practice: MFRs annotated as later sessions clarified earlier answers. "December 28, 2004" is most plausibly a typing or OCR error for 23 December 2003 or 28 January 2004 |
| **EC3** | Not indicated |
| **EC4** | Not indicated. **Recorded explicitly:** nothing here suggests the annotations alter substance adversely; two of the three add material *against* the witness's earlier answer (p.19's note corrects Tenet's account of the March 2000 Principals meeting) |
| **EC5** | Not raised |

**Verification state.** The p.19 note **is** image-verified: the page image shows "December 28, 2004" in italic bracketed text. The p.12 and p.13 footnote dates are from the extracted text layer only and remain open verification tasks; the OCR of this release is demonstrably imperfect, and the p.13 digit is uncertain.

**Recorded as a document property, not as a finding about the Commission's practice.** One document showing later annotation establishes that this document was annotated later. Whether Commission MFRs generally carry undated annotations is a separate question that eight unread MFRs could answer and this one cannot.
