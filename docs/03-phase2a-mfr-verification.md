## Impartiality rule in force

This document, like every document in this repository, is governed by the impartiality rule in [`README.md`](../README.md): the official account and every alternative account are competing hypotheses, none of which receives a presumption of truth, falsity, credibility, or implausibility because it is official, institutional, popular, politically inconvenient, or labeled conspiratorial. All hypotheses are tested against the same evidentiary standards, alternative explanations, predicted observations, and burden of proof. An evidentiary gap — a redaction, an unlocated memorandum, an untested claim — is never itself treated as proof of any hypothesis, official or alternative.

## 1. Formal framing corrections

General rule that now applies to the entire body of the audit, retroactively:

> **Access restriction, generic warning, and interested testimony are not — alone or combined — proof of cover-up or of prior knowledge.**

Each of these three has its own limited evidentiary value: access restriction is a fact about the *process* of an investigation; a generic warning is evidence that a *category* of threat was known; interested testimony is a claim that gains weight from an oath and loses it from interest. None converts into proof of a deliberate act without independent corroboration.

### 1.1 The three-dates rule

Every documentary citation in this audit must now distinguish:

| Date | What it is | Error it avoids |
|---|---|---|
| **Event date** | When the reported fact occurred (meeting, decision, source report) | Confusing the report with the fact |
| **Document date** | When the record was produced | Treating memory reconstructed years later as a contemporaneous record |
| **Declassification date** | When the record became public | **Treating "a new document" as "a new 2001 fact"** |

Applied to the three documents read in Phase 1:

| Doc | Event | Document | Declassification | Event → document gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DOC-1 — *Report on Review of PDB Articles* | PDBs from 1998 to 20/09/2001 (reviewed corpus) | 09/02/2004 | 08/09/2026 | 2.5 to 6 years |
| DOC-2 — MFR Condoleezza Rice | 07/02/2004 (the meeting); facts reported from 2000–2001 | 07/02/2004 (working note) | 08/09/2026 | ~0 for the meeting; 2.5–4 years for the facts |
| DOC-3 — MFR Mike Scheuer | 11/12/2003 (the testimony); facts reported from 1996–1999 | 11/12/2003 | 08/09/2026 | ~0 for the testimony; 4–7 years for the facts |

**Direct consequence.** None of the three documents is a contemporaneous record of the 1998–2001 facts it describes. DOC-2 and DOC-3 are declared memory 2.5 to 7 years removed from the fact; DOC-1 is a synthesis produced in 2004 about 1998–2001 documents. The 2026 declassification made **2003–2004 records** public, not 2001 operational documents. Where evidentiary value depends on contemporaneity — and in priorities 2, 3, and 6 below it does — what matters is the contemporaneous document *cited* within the testimony, not the testimony itself.

| | |
|---|---|

## 4. Phase 2A priorities — pre-registered questions

Pre-registering the question before reading the document is what prevents the after-the-fact adjustment that Phase 1 identified as a methodological failure in audit `01`. Each priority below defines **what would count as a positive, a negative, and an inconclusive answer** before any reading.

### Priority 1 — The al-Mihdhar/al-Hazmi node

The point where H5 has its best theoretical case, and which remains **entirely untouched**.

- **Targets:** MFRs of Tenet (×3) and Clarke (×3); Scheuer #2 (06/01/2004) and #3 (11/03/2004); Joint Inquiry; DOJ IG reports; PENTTBOM.
- **Question:** who knew, on what date, that al-Mihdhar had a valid US visa and that al-Hazmi had entered the US; why the information was not passed to the FBI or added to a watchlist between January 2000 and August 2001; and whether there is a contemporaneous record — not 2003–2004 memory — of a decision to **withhold** the information.
- **Positive answer for H5:** a contemporaneous document showing a deliberate decision not to pass the information along, with identified authorship and no stated operational justification.
- **Negative answer:** a contemporaneous record of a process failure (a cable not sent, a draft not approved, the wrong channel), or an operational justification stated at the time.
- **Inconclusive:** only memory declared years later, with no contemporaneous document located — which is the current state.

**Result [session 2].** Tenet's MFR #3 (28/01/2004) deals directly with the Kuala Lumpur/al-Mihdhar episode ("The Kuala Lumpur (KL) Story"), and Clarke's MFR #2 (12/01/2004) deals directly with the failure to pass the information to Clarke. Neither is the contemporaneous document the test requires — both are memory declared in 2004 about 2000–2001 facts — but both were read in full and move the priority's state from "entirely untouched" to "touched, still inconclusive":

- Tenet disputes the Joint Inquiry's characterization that CIA and FBI were not communicating about the KL case, stating that "FBI was, in fact, far more aware of the Kuala Lumpur meeting than has previously been made public" and that the CIA asked the NSA to put al-Mihdhar on a watchlist ("CIA asked NSA to put Midhar on a watchlist"). There is no specific date for that request, nor an explanation of why formal inclusion on the watchlist occurred only in August 2001.
- Clarke states under oath that he did not know al-Mihdhar and al-Hazmi were present in the US, that Dale Watson (FBI) never mentioned the hunt for the two to him, and that, when he asked Tenet and C/CTC Cofer Black after 9/11 why he had not known, "[they] said that one analyst in CTC who should have put the two on the watchlist did not" — that is, the explanation the CIA's own leadership gave to Clarke, as he relates it, is individual analyst failure, not a deliberate decision to withhold.
- No contemporaneous document (cable, internal 2000–2001 memorandum) was located or cited by author's name in this session. The observed pattern — three witnesses (Clarke, Tenet, Black via Clarke) converging in 2003–2004 on an explanation of individual/process failure — is consistent with the pre-registered **negative answer**, but does not formally satisfy it, because the test requires "a contemporaneous record," and what exists is testimony declared nearly four years later.
- **Priority status: remains inconclusive on the formal test; advances from "untouched" to "touched, with testimonial — not documentary — convergence on a process-failure explanation".** H5 remains without positive evidence found.

### Priority 2 — The 30% vs. 0% discrepancy (capture operation, May 1998)

- **Targets:** MFRs of Tenet and Berger; MFRs of Reno, Freeh, White, and Fitzgerald (**not** part of the 2026 release — location pending in NARA's general MFR holdings).
- **Question:** do the two numbers exist in a contemporaneous record, or only in Scheuer's memory in December 2003? Who communicated which estimate to whom, and on what date?
- **Discriminating test:** if Tenet or Berger describe the same meeting with an estimate different from 30%, the claim moves to `contradicted` or `partially corroborated` depending on the detail; if neither mentions numbers, it remains `uncorroborated statement`.

**Result [session 2].** The discriminating test was applied and splits claim A3 into two parts with opposite outcomes — see the breakdown in A3a/A3b in table §5:

- **The existence of an internal success estimate below 30% is now corroborated by a source independent of the witness.** Tenet's MFR #2 (22/01/2004) states, about the same May 1998 operation: "the chance of success... described to him was less than 30% (the figure agreed upon [by the CIA's approval chain])". This matches what Scheuer's own MFR #2 (06/01/2004, read in this session) records as the Agency's internal approval figure: "It was agreed that the plan had a 20-30% chance of complete success," with the Islamabad station chief estimating 50-60%. Tenet and Scheuer are distinct declarants, each read in full, describing the same number for the same operation — the instrument's `corroborated` standard (§5, application rules) is met **for the existence of the number**, not for who it was communicated to.
- **The claim that 30% was specifically communicated to Berger, Reno, Freeh, and Clarke finds no support in any of the nine MFRs, and Berger directly denies having received any estimate.** Berger's MFR (14/01/2004) is explicit: "Berger does not remember ever being given estimates or the plan's chances for success" and "the operation was never presented to the White House for a decision." Tenet #2's account of having called Berger confirms the call took place, but was to inform him of a cancellation decision already made — "he did not ask for Berger's opinion... rather called Berger to inform him of his operational decision" — with no mention of percentages. Clarke's MFRs (×3) describe the cancellation process in detail (Berger asked Tenet whether he approved the plan; Tenet said he did not) but likewise mention no number communicated to Clarke. Because no independent source confirms that a number was given to these people, there is no frontal contradiction of a specific figure, so the correct status under the closed vocabulary is `uncorroborated statement`, not `contradicted`; Berger's direct denial of **having received any estimate at all** is the strongest finding against this part of the claim.
- **The claim of "0% told to Mary Jo White" remains entirely uncorroborated, and Scheuer's own MFR #2 reveals that Scheuer's source for that figure was, already in 2003, secondhand.** The text: "it was relayed back to Scheuer by his contacts in New York that C/CTC Geoff O'Connell had briefed the operation to the Attorney's office of the Southern District of New York as having 0% chance of success." This is not Scheuer testifying to something he witnessed; it is something "relayed back" to him by "contacts in New York," about a briefing to the SDNY "Attorney's office" in general — not specifically to Mary Jo White. This is not a contradiction of what `02`/DOC-3 already recorded (Phase 1 already logged the claim as coming from Scheuer), but it is an important refinement: the information's chain of custody, in the primary source, is witness → unidentified third-party rumor → characterization attributed to O'Connell. It remains `uncorroborated statement`.

### Priority 3 — The allegedly withheld 25/03/1999 PDB

- **Targets:** the memorandum cited by Scheuer; the author of the removed text, named in the MFR; Tenet's MFRs.
- **Methodological trap to avoid:** the published 25/03/1999 PDB — if it is among the 71 released — **cannot** confirm or refute the claim, because the claim is about a text that would have been *removed before publication*. An item absent from a PDB is compatible with both "it was removed" and "it was never written." The real test is the internal memorandum and the named author's testimony.
- **Current status:** `document cited, not located`.

**Result [session 2].** Tenet's three MFRs — the only ones, among the nine, whose declarant could speak with authority about a DCI decision to remove text from a PDB — were read in full and **contain no mention whatsoever** of a 25/03/1999 PDB, of removed WMD-related text, or of a decision to hide a delay in passing information to the FBI. This is a negative search, not a refutation: the Commission's interviewers simply did not raise the subject with Tenet in these three sessions (23/12/2003, 22/01/2004, 28/01/2004), and the internal memorandum cited by Scheuer remains unlocated. **Status unchanged: `document cited, not located`.**

### Priority 4 — The 06/08/2001 PDB

- **Target:** the PDB's PDF on the CIA's page. Technical obstacle already documented: a scan with no text layer. Requires OCR — and therefore requires access to the file.
- **Question:** does the full text support Rice's characterization ("no current information"; the UAE embassy contact as a hoax) or the Commission's description in DOC-1 (70 FBI field investigations; CIA and FBI investigating the contact)?
- **Note:** both things can be true at once. "Historical, not current, information" and "there are 70 open investigations" do not necessarily contradict each other. The test is the text.

**Result [session 2] — priority resolved.** The technical obstacle was overcome: the 06/08/2001 PDF has no native text layer, but OCR (`pdftoppm` 300 DPI + `tesseract`) produced running, legible, internally coherent text across its two pages. The full text **supports DOC-1's description and contradicts Rice's characterization**:

> "The FBI is conducting approximately 70 full field investigations throughout the US that it considers Bin Ladin-related. CIA and the FBI are investigating a call to our Embassy in the UAE in May saying that a group of Bin Ladin supporters was in the US planning attacks with explosives."

The document does not call the UAE embassy contact a hoax — it treats it as the object of an active joint CIA-FBI investigation. The non-corroboration caveat the PDB actually contains concerns a different, older item (a 1998 report about hijacking a plane to free the "Blind Shaykh"): "We have not been able to corroborate some of the more sensational threat reporting, such as that from a [redacted] service in 1998 saying that Bin Ladin wanted to hijack a US aircraft...". Nowhere in the document does it state that the information was purely historical with no current relevance — on the contrary, the PDB opens by stating that Bin Ladin "since 1997 has wanted to conduct terrorist attacks in the US" and closes with the two active investigations cited above.

- **A10 moves from `inconclusive` to `contradicted`** (the contradicting source — the PDB itself — read in full, as the instrument's application rule requires).
- **A16 moves from `document located, not read` to `corroborated`**: the PDB Review Team's characterization (DOC-1) that the document carried a caveat of non-corroboration for the more sensational reports is confirmed by the text now read — although it is not possible to confirm in this session whether the caveat was, in the original, in bold (emphasis formatting does not survive OCR reliably).

### Priority 6 — CIA–FBI interaction and the gap between foreign intelligence and domestic investigation

- **Targets:** MFRs of Tenet and Clarke; Joint Inquiry; DOJ IG reports.
- **Question:** was the gap (i) a legal barrier ("the wall"), (ii) institutional practice beyond what the barrier required, or (iii) a case-by-case decision to withhold? The three produce the same observable outcome and have completely different implications for H1, H4, and H5.
- **Why it matters:** this is the distinction that separates H4 from H5 in the concrete case. Treating them as one was the category error Phase 1 made with the four demolition hypotheses, and it must not be repeated here.

**Result [session 2] — priority partially resolved: (i) and (ii) coexist, with cross-corroboration between two independent witnesses.**

- **(i) A formal legal barrier, identified and named.** Clarke #1 (18/12/2003) describes Federal Rule 6E (the grand jury rule) as the formal obstacle the FBI cited for not sharing information with the White House: "the FBI said the White House couldn't see it because of Federal Rule 6E, the grand jury rule." Reno proposed working around this by showing material to the NSC before the grand jury did, but the formal NSC-DOJ agreement "went into perpetual negotiations and was never finished."
- **(ii) Institutional practice beyond the formal barrier, corroborated by two independent witnesses who describe the same concrete deficiencies.** Clarke #1 and Tenet #2 (22/01/2004) — read in full, distinct declarants, with neither citing the other on this specific point — converge on the same list of FBI institutional deficiencies, not stemming from Rule 6E: the absence of a dedicated counterterrorism headquarters (Tenet: "the Bureau had no CT headquarters"), the absence of reports officers to produce analysis from the 302s (Clarke: "FBI has no reports-officer mechanism for analytical comment"; Tenet, in almost the same words: "the FBI lacked reports officers, the ability to link cases, spot trends and do analyses, and did not disseminate intelligence"), and difficulty accessing the FBI's own 302s even for the CIA (Tenet: "It was hard to access FBI's 302s"). Convergence between two witnesses who were not coordinating on this specific part of their testimony satisfies the instrument's `corroborated` rule (§5) for the narrow proposition: **the limitations went beyond the formal legal barrier and included the institution's own analytic-capacity and dissemination deficits.**
- **(iii) A case-by-case decision to withhold specific information** was neither corroborated nor refuted by any of the nine MFRs as a general explanation — but it did appear as a specific explanation in the concrete al-Mihdhar/al-Hazmi case (see Priority 1, result): there, the explanation given was individual analyst failure, not a legal barrier or a deliberate decision.
- **Priority conclusion:** the CIA-FBI gap does not have a single cause. (i) and (ii) operated simultaneously and are distinguishable in the primary source — the formal barrier (Rule 6E) explains why the White House did not receive grand-jury material in writing; the FBI's institutional deficits (no reports officers, no CT headquarters, no dissemination) explain why even information outside Rule 6E's scope did not circulate well. Neither, alone or combined, amounts to a "case-by-case decision to withhold" in the sense of item (iii) — which remains uncorroborated as a general pattern.

## 5. Claim-by-claim verification instrument

Closed status vocabulary. No other term is admitted in this column:

`uncorroborated statement` · `document cited, not located` · `document located, not read` · `partially corroborated` · `corroborated` · `contradicted` · `inconclusive`

Application rules:

- `corroborated` requires a **source independent of the declarant**, read in full. A declarant corroborating himself in another statement corroborates nothing.
- `document cited, not located` is the default status for any claim resting on a memorandum, cable, or report the audit has not located — even under oath, even with a date and addressee.
- `contradicted` requires that the contradicting source have been read in full. Tension between two descriptions not read in parallel is `inconclusive`, not `contradicted`.

## 6. Phase 2B — Joint Inquiry, Part Four: pre-registered protocol

**Not executed.** In session 1, `intelligence.senate.gov` was blocked (section 3). **In session 2 (19/09/2026), access to that domain was tested and confirmed (HTTP 200)** — see §3.1 — but the reading of Part Four was not performed in this session; it fell outside the executed scope, which focused on the nine MFRs and the PDB (Phase 2A). The protocol below remains pre-registered for the session that performs the reading.

### 6.1 Treatment rule

> The "28 pages" are **not** proof of Saudi state participation, and will not be treated as such under any circumstance.

The document is, by construction, a chapter about **information not pursued to the end** — an inventory of what the Joint Inquiry found and did not resolve. An item not pursued is a pending lead, not a suppressed conclusion. Three distinctions the protocol keeps separate in every line:

1. **An individual with a government tie ≠ the government.** Employment, a stipend, or a consular credential establish a tie, not authorization.
2. **Contact ≠ support ≠ conscious support for the attack.** Each step requires its own evidence.
3. **An investigative lead ≠ an investigative conclusion.** Part Four mostly records the former; saying which is which is the product of the reading.

### 6.2 Classification instrument

Each item in Part Four receives exactly one category:

| Category | Operational definition | Evidentiary weight |
|---|---|---|
| **lead** | An investigative line recorded, with no stated resolution | Directs future search. Zero as evidence |
| **statement** | A claim by an identified or unidentified person, with no documentary corroboration in the text | Depends on identification, interest, and corroboration |
| **document** | A record cited with date and origin | Weight according to contemporaneity and provenance |
| **corroborated fact** | A statement the text declares verified by two or more independent sources | Highest available in the document |
| **judicial allegation** | An allegation from a court filing (*In re Terrorist Attacks* and related cases) | An interested party's allegation. **Never** treated as an investigative finding |
| **investigative conclusion** | An explicit judgment by the Joint Inquiry itself | The body's weight, limited by what the body states it verified |

Each line also records: whether the Joint Inquiry declared the item **resolved, unresolved, or not pursued**; and whether there is any indication that the FBI or CIA followed up.

### 6.3 Pre-registered question

At the end of the reading, three questions, answered only with what the text supports:

1. How many items are `corroborated fact` or `investigative conclusion`, versus how many are `lead` or `statement`?
2. Does any item establish **authorization or knowledge by an authority of the Saudi government**, as opposed to conduct by individuals with a government tie?
3. Is what Part Four says about al-Bayoumi, al-Thumairy, and Bassnan conscious support for the attack, documented contact, or an unpursued lead — item by item?

**None of these questions is answered in this session.** H2 and H3 remain where Phase 1 left them: genuinely unresolved.
