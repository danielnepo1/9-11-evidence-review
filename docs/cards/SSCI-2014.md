### 1.1 Source character — stated plainly

This document is the Committee's own synthesis of CIA operational cables (cited by heavily redacted report numbers, e.g. "ALEC ▮▮▮▮ (021242Z DEC 02)"), CIA emails, and CIA Inspector General material. It is **not** the underlying cable traffic itself, which was not sought or read in this project. Where this card quotes SSCI stating a fact, it is cited as "SSCI, with access to the cable traffic, reports that..." — the same posture this project takes toward `DOJOIG-2004` and `JI-2002`.

## 3. Declared source of each assertion

SSCI declares its own source for every proposition above: internal CIA cable traffic, cited by (largely redacted) cable identifiers and date-time groups in the footnotes (e.g. fn. 335–340 on PDF p. 96). No proposition used in this card rests on an SSCI assertion without a cited cable reference, though the cable itself was not independently retrieved.

---

## 4. What the document does not say

- **It does not state an EIT-cessation date for Hambali.** The latest EIT-context cable found (Nov. 30 / Dec. 1, 2003) is a documented floor, not a stated ceiling — SSCI nowhere says EITs against Hambali stopped on or by any specific date. This absence is load-bearing for `12` §3 and D-009's `PRE-EIT`/`INCONCLUSIVE-NSC` categories.
- **It does not state the exact day of Hambali's August 2003 capture** — the day is redacted at every one of the six corroborating passages found.
- **It does not state Nashiri's exact rendition date to CIA custody** — "November [redacted], 2002" at PDF p. 96; only the month is legible.
- **It does not itself discuss `CR-2004` or any citation within it** — this document does not corroborate or contradict any specific Commission Report endnote; the cross-reference in `12` is this project's construction, not SSCI's.

---

## 5. Temporal limits

The document covers the CIA's detention-and-interrogation program, 2001–2009. The nine pages read concern the period September 2002 – October 2004 specifically. It does not cover anything before September 2001 or after 2009. The document itself (2014) is produced roughly 10–12 years after the events it describes — the same order of retrospective distance as `CR-2004` itself, but from cable traffic rather than post-hoc detainee interviews.

---

## 6. Uncertainty language

- "**approximately** a month after his transfer" (S4) — SSCI itself hedges the EIT-approval timing for Hambali.
- "**approximately** three additional weeks after this exchange" (S7) — SSCI hedges bin al-Shibh's cessation estimate; this project in turn treats the resulting March 2003 cessation date as an estimate, not a fixed fact (`12` §2).
- "**mid**-October 2002" (S5, Nashiri) — SSCI does not give an exact day.
- No hedge at all attaches to S4's core fact ("Hambali was captured and transferred to CIA custody" in August 2003) or to S6's four specific EIT date ranges for Nashiri — these are stated flatly.

---

## 7. Redactions and missing parts

Every cable identifier, most detention-site country names, and several exact calendar days (Hambali's capture day; Nashiri's DETENTION SITE COBALT/GREEN transfer days; bin Attash's exact rendition day in May 2003) are redacted throughout the nine pages read. **None of the redactions read change the apparent meaning of any proposition relied on in `12`** — in every case, the redacted item is a sub-day or an identifying detail, not the month/year-level fact this project's classification depends on.

## 9. B2 binary determination

**Not applicable in the usual sense** — this card is itself the *timeline source* used to classify the 54 `CR-2004` notes already determined `B2-rely=yes, CIA-DETENTION` in `11`. `SSCI-2014` is not itself an endnote source being classified for B2; it is the instrument used to date-classify others.

---

## 10. Evidentiary ceiling

`SSCI-2014` establishes, for the six named detainees, **when** SSCI itself documents them as subjected to the CIA's enhanced interrogation techniques. It does not establish, and is not used to establish: whether any specific `CR-2004` chapter 5 assertion is true; whether information given during or after an EIT-application window is more or less reliable than SSCI's own timing suggests; or anything about detainees not named in this card. Per `prereg/B2-ch5-EIT-timing.md` §5, a timing classification derived from this card is a provenance fact, never a reliability verdict.
