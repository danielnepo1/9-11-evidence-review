### 6.1 Typographic emphasis — read from the page image, not the OCR

The document uses **bold italic for the lead sentence of each paragraph**, with the remainder in roman. This is the article's standard layout convention throughout.

**P8 is the exception: the entire paragraph is bold italic, not merely its lead sentence.** Every other paragraph on p.1 reverts to roman after its opening sentence; the non-corroboration paragraph does not.

This is recorded as an exact typographic fact with two readings, both admissible and neither adopted here:
- the paragraph is short enough that its lead sentence *is* the paragraph, so the emphasis may be nothing more than the standing convention;
- or the emphasis is deliberate and the caveat was meant to stand out.

The image does not settle which. **The characterisation "in bold in the original" is confirmed as literally accurate; whether that bold carries special weight is not determinable from the document alone.**

## 10. Evidentiary ceiling

**What this document can support:**

- That on 6 August 2001 the President was given a two-page article asserting a standing intent to strike in the US, resting on the source mix in §3.
- That the article contained current-state items (P9, P10) alongside historical ones.
- That the article contained an explicit non-corroboration caveat scoped to "some of the more sensational threat reporting" (P8), set entirely in bold italic.
- That the article named no target, date, method, operative or recommendation (§4).

**What this document cannot support:**

- That anyone did or did not act reasonably on it. Reasonableness is a judgement about a decision process, and this document is not a record of one.
- That a warning of the 9/11 attacks was given. Nothing in the text describes the attack method, targets or timing (§4).
- That a warning was absent. The document explicitly reports patterns "consistent with preparations for hijackings" (P9), and absence of specificity is not absence of warning.
- Any conclusion about Rice's characterisations. Those require her statements read in the primary, plus the dating of the hoax assessment — neither done (§8).
- Any conclusion about the reliability of the reporting behind P3, P4 and P8, because the services are redacted (§7).
