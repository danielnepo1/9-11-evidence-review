### 1.1 Source character and genealogy — the decisive fact about this document

Note 44 (below) is the Commission's entire treatment of the false-record episode, and **its cited sources are the DOJ Inspector General's**: "DOJ Inspector General interview of Doug M., Feb. 12, 2004; DOJ Inspector General interview of Michael, Oct. 31, 2002; CIA cable, Jan. 5, 2000; DOJ Inspector General report, 'A Review of the FBI's Handling of Intelligence Information Related to the 9/11 Attacks,' **July 2, 2004**, p. 282."

The Commission therefore drew on a **July 2004 draft** of the OIG report that this audit read in its November 2004 / June 2005 form. **`CR-2004` and `DOJOIG-2004` share an ancestor on this episode and are not independent.** Only the January 5 cable itself is cited directly.

## 6. Uncertainty language

| Marker | Where |
|---|---|
| "**alleging**" | C4 — the Commission's word for the cable's assertion |
| "**contradict**" | C4 — stronger than the OIG's "we found no evidence that this cable was correct" |
| "we conclude this was not the case" | C9 — a flat finding against testimony |
| "even when there is mutual goodwill" | C10 — the Commission's own ordinary-explanation framing |
| "we do not attribute much significance to this failure alone" | note 53, on the March 2000 delay |

**Wording strength differs across the three bodies on the same record.** JI (2002): the CIA "was unable to confirm". OIG (2004): "no evidence that this cable was correct". Commission (2004): "Contemporaneous documents **contradict** the claim." In this project's own vocabulary those are `inconclusive`, `unsupported statement` and `contradicted` respectively — three different statuses for one proposition, assigned by three bodies. Recorded as a fact about the reconstructions.

## 10. Evidentiary ceiling

**What the pages read can support:** that the Commission, drawing on the DOJ OIG's draft and interviews, recorded the false-record episode in a footnote in terms stronger than the OIG's own; that it attributed the hold to "a CIA desk officer" without mentioning the Deputy Chief; that it dated the cable 5 January 2000; and that in a separate 2001 episode it found CIA Joint-Inquiry testimony of sharing to be untrue.

**What they cannot support:** independence from the OIG on this episode — there is none; any inference from the body's silence about the CIR (a report can reasonably confine a record-level detail to a note); anything about the Deputy Chief's role, which the Commission does not address; any of the 575 pages not read.
