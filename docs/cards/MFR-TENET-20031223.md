### 6.1 The bracketed divergence (T18) is the document's most unusual feature

The square-bracketed sentence is the staff's own interpolation. Two Commission staff members sat in the same meeting and recorded **different understandings of which President and which year** the DCI was describing — Bush in summer 2001, or Clinton at the end of 1999.

Recorded exactly as what it is: **a contemporaneous, documented instance of two trained note-takers diverging on the meaning of the same statement.** It is registered as `conflicting testimony` material for the contradiction ledger, with the note that the conflict is *internal to the record* and was disclosed by its own authors rather than discovered later.

Its significance is method, not motive: the same statement, differently heard, would place the "5 to 15 attacks in the United States" prediction either inside the summer-2001 threat period or two years before it. Those are materially different propositions. **The document does not settle which, and neither does this card.**

**Census rule established by this document, and it is not a detail.** The B2 question as posed — "does this note cite or reference interrogation-derived reporting?" — returns YES for a document that draws no substantive conclusion from such reporting at all. A binary that cannot tell *reliance* from *discussion* would count this the same as a claim about the plot sourced entirely to a detainee.

The census therefore requires **two columns, never totalled together**:

| Column | Definition |
|---|---|
| **B2-ref** | The text mentions or discusses detainee/interrogation-derived reporting |
| **B2-rely** | A factual proposition in the text **rests** on detainee/interrogation-derived reporting |

This document is **B2-ref = yes, B2-rely = no**. `PDB-20010806` is **B2-ref = yes, B2-rely = yes** (proposition P5, on a custodial/cooperating-witness statement, which is itself a third subtype).

## 10. Evidentiary ceiling

**What this document can support:**

- That on 23 December 2003 the DCI refused the Commission direct access to seven individuals it considered central to the plot, that the refusal was stated as firm, that reasons were given, and that alternatives short of physical access were offered (T2–T12). This is **first-hand, contemporaneous to the meeting, and recorded by a participant**.
- That the Commission asked for certification of completed document production and was offered certification of a good-faith effort (T14–T15).
- That the CIA had conceded 9/11-plot experts were not conducting the interrogations, as asserted by Zelikow in the meeting and not contradicted in the record of it (T10).
- That two Commission staff present recorded different understandings of the same statement by the DCI (T18).

**What this document cannot support:**

- Anything about the substance of 9/11. It contains no factual account of the plot, the warnings, the agencies' pre-attack conduct, or any operative.
- That access was ultimately denied. The document records a refusal **on that date**, in a meeting whose own participants expected it ("The Vice Chair said that he had expected the DCI to turn down this request") and left open ("At least for today"). What followed is not in this document.
- That document production was incomplete. T15 records what the CIA offered to certify, not what it produced.
- Any inference of concealment. A refusal with stated operational reasons is a refusal with stated operational reasons; under `04` §2.1 an access restriction is a fact about an investigation's process and is not evidence of what was concealed. Establishing more requires evidence that the stated reasons were not the operative ones — which this document does not contain in either direction.
- Anything about Cofer Black's prediction, its audience or its date, because the document's own authors could not agree on it (T18).
