## 1. Governing principle

The following rule is adopted verbatim and governs every phase, every register line and every future document in this repository:

> “Every material claim is provisional until supported by traceable evidence. Official status does not make a claim true; institutional inconsistency does not make an alternative claim true. The project applies skepticism symmetrically: it actively tests the official account for contradictions and unexplained anomalies, while applying the same burden of proof, alternative-explanation testing, and falsification criteria to every alternative hypothesis.”

### 1.1 What this commits the project to

1. The project **does** actively investigate contradictions, anomalies, unexplained events, evidentiary gaps, changed accounts and unusual procedural decisions inside the official account. Declining to look is a methodological failure, not caution.
2. The project **does not** assume the official account is false, and **does not** assume any alternative account is false. The official account and every alternative account are competing explanations, tested against the same record.
3. The official account is not a single protected object. It is a large set of separable claims produced by different institutions at different times with different evidentiary support. Some of its propositions may be strongly supported while others in the same document are weakly supported or unsupported; the audit must be able to say so claim by claim.
4. An alternative hypothesis gains no support from the weakness of an official claim. It gains support only from positive, traceable evidence for what it itself asserts.

## 2. Rules retained from earlier phases

These remain in force and are restated here so that `04` is self-contained.

### 2.1 Evidentiary framing rule (from `03` §1)

> Access restriction, generic warning and interested testimony are not — singly or in combination — proof of a cover-up or of prior knowledge.

### 2.2 The three-dates rule (from `03` §1.1)

Every documentary citation distinguishes **event date**, **document date** and **declassification date**. A 2004 record declassified in 2026 is 2004 memory, not a contemporaneous 2001 record.

### 2.3 Each step requires its own evidence (from `03` §1)

Process failure → institutional self-protection → deliberate suppression → operational prior knowledge are four distinct propositions. Evidence for one is never promoted to evidence for the next.

### 2.4 Reading is reading (from `02`/`03`)

A summary, FAQ, press release, search snippet or third-party news report does not support a status of `corroborated` or `contradicted`, and does not count as having read a primary source.

### 2.5 Symmetric extension of 2.1–2.4

The same four rules apply in the opposite direction. An alternative hypothesis may not be supported by: an unanswered question, a refused FOIA, a destroyed record, a witness who changed their account, or an unusual procedural decision — unless the specific mechanism that hypothesis asserts is itself evidenced.

## 3. Symmetry requirements

These are binding constraints on every inference in every phase.

| # | Requirement |
|---|---|
| S1 | **H0 must be decomposed.** The official account is never scored as a single unit. It is broken into atomic propositions (§4), each carrying its own evidentiary status. A phase report that assigns one status to "the official account" is non-compliant. |
| S2 | **H1–H7 gain nothing from gaps.** No alternative hypothesis is strengthened by an evidentiary gap, by institutional opacity, or by distrust of an institution. Only positive evidence for its asserted mechanism moves it. |
| S3 | **Institutional failure ≠ deliberate non-intervention.** Evidence that an institution failed is evidence of failure. Promoting it to evidence of intent requires separate, direct evidence of that intent. |
| S4 | **Contradiction ≠ material intervention.** Evidence that two accounts conflict is evidence of a conflict. It is not, by itself, evidence that anyone intervened in the physical event. |
| S5 | **Absence of a test is a limitation, not a result.** "X was never tested" is recorded under limitations. It is never recorded as evidence for or against any hypothesis. |
| S6 | **Burden scales with the assertion, not the source.** What a claim must clear depends on what it asserts — its extraordinariness, the number of actors it requires, the coordination it implies — not on whether it came from a government report or from a critic. |
| S7 | **Both directions of error are named.** Every phase report must state what it could be wrong about in favour of the official account *and* what it could be wrong about in favour of an alternative. |

## 4. Atomic claim decomposition

Every official claim entering the Stress-Test Track is decomposed into atomic propositions. An atomic proposition is one that can be true or false independently of the others in its set; if a sentence can be half-true, it is not yet atomic.

### 4.1 Required fields

For every atomic proposition, record all twelve fields. A field that cannot be filled is written as `not determined` — never left blank, never filled from prior knowledge.

| # | Field | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Exact claim** | The atomic proposition, quoted or minimally paraphrased. No compound statements. |
| C2 | **Responsible institution or witness** | Who asserts it. For an institution, name the document and the office that issued it; for a witness, name the person. |
| C3 | **Original source and date** | The source in which the claim first appears, with event date, document date and declassification date (§2.2). |
| C4 | **Source character** | One of: `contemporaneous`, `retrospective`, `sworn`, `technical`, `secondary`. More than one may apply (e.g. sworn + retrospective); list all that apply. |
| C5 | **Later revisions, retractions, conflicting accounts** | Every subsequent change to the claim by the same source, and every account by another source that conflicts with it, each with its own date. |
| C6 | **Assumptions required for the claim to hold** | The premises that must be true for the claim to be true, including modelling assumptions, instrument assumptions and institutional-behaviour assumptions. |
| C7 | **Evidence supporting it** | Traceable items, with read-status for each (per §2.4). |
| C8 | **Evidence contradicting or weakening it** | Same standard. Distinguish *contradicting* (incompatible) from *weakening* (reduces support without excluding). |
| C9 | **Plausible non-conspiratorial explanations** | Error, memory decay, classification practice, bureaucratic routine, incomplete records, ordinary forensic uncertainty, institutional incentive to look competent. |
| C10 | **Plausible alternative explanations** | Explanations requiring intent, coordination or concealment. State which of H1–H7 each maps to, and what it would require to be true. |
| C11 | **Most discriminating missing item** | The **single** document, test or data point that would most reduce uncertainty between C9 and C10. One item, named specifically. |
| C12 | **Current status** | From the closed vocabulary in §4.2. |

### 4.2 Status vocabulary for atomic propositions

Closed vocabulary. No other term is admitted:

`unsupported statement` · `document cited, not located` · `document located, not read` · `partially corroborated` · `corroborated` · `contradicted` · `inconclusive`

Application rules (carried over from `03` §5 and extended):

- `corroborated` requires a source **independent of the claimant**, read in full. A witness corroborating themselves in another statement corroborates nothing.
- `contradicted` requires the contradicting source to have been read in full. Tension between two sources not read in parallel is `inconclusive`.
- These same rules apply to alternative-account claims, without exception.

The working register is [`claim-decomposition-register.md`](claim-decomposition-register.md). Each entry uses this template:

## 5. Anomaly classification

The register is [`anomaly-register.md`](anomaly-register.md); its field definitions live there. The statuses and the mirror test are defined here because they are methodology, not bookkeeping.

### 5.1 Status vocabulary

Closed vocabulary. Exactly one status per anomaly:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `documentary contradiction` | Two documents assert incompatible things about the same fact. |
| `timeline discrepancy` | Two records place the same event at incompatible times, or an interval is inconsistent with a stated sequence. |
| `conflicting testimony` | Two or more accounts by people conflict on a material point. |
| `unexplained procedural deviation` | A documented departure from an established rule, routine or standard practice, with no explanation on the record. |
| `missing or destroyed evidence` | Material known to have existed that is now absent, withheld, or destroyed. |
| `chain-of-custody limitation` | Material exists but its handling record is incomplete enough to limit what can be concluded from it. |
| `model assumption or non-replicability issue` | A technical result depends on assumptions not independently verified, or inputs not released, or cannot be reproduced. |
| `physical or forensic anomaly` | An observation about physical evidence that is not accounted for by the stated explanation. |
| `apparent anomaly with adequate explanation` | Examined and resolved by an ordinary explanation. Retained in the register — resolved anomalies are results. |
| `unresolved anomaly` | Examined, not resolved, and not yet assignable to a more specific status. |

### 5.2 What an anomaly is

> **An anomaly is a research lead. By itself it is not evidence of intent, foreknowledge, complicity, or intervention.**

This applies in both directions. An anomaly in the official account does not support H1–H7 on its own; an anomaly in an alternative account's reasoning does not confirm H0 on its own.

### 5.3 The mirror test

No anomaly may be assigned a final status until all five questions are answered on the record. `not determined` is an acceptable answer; silence is not.

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| M1 | What would the official account predict? |
| M2 | What would each relevant alternative hypothesis predict? |
| M3 | What observation would discriminate between them? |
| M4 | Is the anomaly explained by error, memory, classification, bureaucracy, incomplete records, or ordinary forensic uncertainty? |
| M5 | What conclusion is **not** justified by the available evidence? |

M5 is mandatory and is written as an explicit prohibition, e.g. "this does not establish that anyone knew X." Its purpose is to stop the register from being quoted as support for a conclusion it does not carry.

### 5.4 Discriminating power

Each anomaly records a discriminating-power rating, derived from M3:

| Rating | Criterion |
|---|---|
| `high` | A specific, identified, plausibly obtainable observation would separate the hypotheses. |
| `medium` | A discriminating observation exists in principle but access or feasibility is unclear. |
| `low` | No observation currently identified would separate the hypotheses; the anomaly is compatible with all of them. |

`low` anomalies are retained but are explicitly non-probative. They may not be cited in any synthesis as support for anything.

## 6. Required reporting format for every future phase

Every phase document from Phase 2A onward must contain these four sections, in this order, with these headings. A phase report missing any of them is non-compliant and is not merged into the synthesis.

1. **Findings that challenge or weaken the official account**
2. **Findings that challenge or weaken alternative accounts**
3. **What remains unresolved**
4. **What evidence would change the assessment**

Rules for each:

- Sections 1 and 2 are both mandatory even when one of them is empty. If empty, it is written as "No findings in this phase weakened [the official account / alternative accounts]," which is itself a reportable result and a signal to check for one-sided reading.
- Section 3 lists open questions with the register IDs (`C-<id>`, `A-<id>`) they attach to. It distinguishes *unresolved because untested* from *unresolved because tested and indeterminate*.
- Section 4 names specific, obtainable items — a named document, a named test, a named dataset — and states, for each, which way it would move which hypothesis. Generic wishes ("more transparency") are not admissible entries.
