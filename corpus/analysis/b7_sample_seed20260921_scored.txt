DIAGNOSIS
- 17 of 18 disagreements share one cause: the implementation of the citation-context rule
  (prereg/B7.md section 3) accepted only a closing quote, a closing paren or a listed
  document-type noun before the date. The pre-registration also lists AUTHOR and
  INSTITUTION as valid contexts; the code omitted them. Person names, case names, unit
  designations and subject lines followed by a comma or an open paren were therefore
  rejected, and the note fell into "no-date" (682 of 1,657 notes — 41% — against 93%
  carrying a parseable date). This is an implementation error against the pre-registered
  rule, not a defect in the rule.
- 1 of 18 (#25) is a min-selection error caused by the same rejection.
- 0 of 18 involve a date inside a quoted title being wrongly accepted (that rule works),
  and 0 involve a narrative date being wrongly accepted as a document date.
- Direction: the rejected dates span 1998-2004, so the error is not one-sided; but it
  empties the denominator, which is enough to void the counts.
- Limitation confirmed independent of the bug (#14, #27): contemporaneous records that
  carry no year token (audio files, "Commission analysis of 911/PAPD calls") are invisible
  to a date-based metric and will inflate "no-date" legitimately.

ACTION
- Fix: implement the pre-registered rule as written — a date counts unless it is inside a
  quoted title or is immediately preceded (no comma or paren between) by a narrative word
  (in, on, since, by, until, before, after, during, of, the, from, to, at, early, late,
  mid, year, ...). Citation dates are preceded by a comma, an open paren, or a name.
